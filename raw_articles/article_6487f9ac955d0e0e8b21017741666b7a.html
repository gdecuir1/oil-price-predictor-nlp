Global Oil Stocks Near Crisis Levels, Prices Could Surge in June: Experts

Finance|
Published 2026.05.04. 18:01:38
|
By Nam Yoon-jung
|

Warnings are mounting that global oil inventories will fall to dangerous levels if the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz continues, triggering a "crisis" in which prices surge severely within a month.

According to the UK daily Financial Times on the 3rd (local time), oil industry executives and analysts warned that by the end of this month, global inventories of crude oil, gasoline, diesel and jet fuel will drop to perilously low levels, causing prices to soar.

Frederic Lasserre, head of research at oil trading firm Gunvor, said, "We don't have months," warning that "enormous pain" could arrive, forcing countries to halt fuel use.

"It goes beyond simple gasoline prices at the pump — industries shut down and we enter a recession," he said. "That inflection point is June."

Amrita Sen, founder of consultancy Energy Aspects, said that if the war continues through the end of June, all inventories will run dry. "Oil prices will be whatever you ask for. There is no buffer," she said. "We expect a serious upside for both crude and petroleum products."

She forecast Brent futures at $150 to $200 per barrel. Brent for June delivery rose to as high as $126 intraday this week before falling back below $110.

Helima Croft, head of global commodity strategy at RBC Capital Markets, noted, "People are starting to realize that the message the US is sending may not reflect reality."

She predicted that if the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz continues this month, oil prices will break through the 2022 peak of nearly $140 per barrel.

According to US Energy Information Administration (EIA) data, the US is releasing 1 million barrels per day from its Strategic Petroleum Reserve, but as of the 24th of last month, gasoline inventories stood at 222 million barrels — the lowest for this time of year in a decade.

One oil industry executive said, "Once US gasoline inventories pass the 210 million barrel mark, it gets ugly," adding, "We're almost at the point where you can see parts of the market really getting twisted."
