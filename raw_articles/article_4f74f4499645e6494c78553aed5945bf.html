Iranian media on Thursday evening reported several explosions along Iran's Gulf coast, including on an island in the Strait of Hormuz.

Iran says it is still considering US proposal conveyed by Pakistani mediators

An Iranian government spokesman has told state media that Tehran is still reviewing the latest terms presented to it by the US and that it was yet to reach a decision or convey a response. 

Foreign Ministry spokesman Email Baghaei was quoted by the IRNA news agency as saying that Iran "has not yet reached a conclusion, and no response has been given to the US side." 

Pakistan, which is mediating the talks between Washington and Tehran, said it was hopeful of a relatively swift agreement seeking an end to hostilities. 

"We expect an agreement sooner than later," Pakistan Foreign Ministry spokesman Tahir Andrabi said on Thursday. "We hope the parties will reach a peaceful and sustainable solution that will contribute not only to peace in our region but to international peace as well." 

President Donald Trump has frequently seemed to change course this week. Late on Sunday, he announced a military operation designed to thwart Iran's bid to block the Strait of Hormuz and help commercial ships through, calling this Project Freedom. 

The military tried to start to enforce this on Monday, seemingly with only limited success amid reports of low-level naval clashes and attacks on the UAE and on commercial ships. 

Within 48 hours of launching the plan, Trump announced that Project Freedom would be paused to allow for the submission of new truce terms.

Skip next section US issues new Iran-related sanctions
05/07/2026May 7, 2026

US issues new Iran-related sanctions

The US Department of the Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) announced a new round of sanctions to individuals and entities tied to Iran, vowing "increased economic pressure" on the Islamic republic and "its proxy militias in Iraq."

"Treasury will not stand idly by as Iran's military exploits Iraqi oil to fund terrorism against the United States and our partners," said Secretary of the Treasury Scott Bessent. 

The list included four who were sanctioned as individuals and among them were Iraq's deputy oil minister ​Ali Maarij Al-Bahadly.

The Treasury Department accused Al-Bahadly of abusing "his position to ​facilitate the diversion of oil ​to ‌be sold for the benefit of the Iranian regime and ‌its proxy militias in Iraq."

The three other individuals are considered to be senior leaders of Iran-aligned militias Kata'ib Sayyid Al-Shuhada and ​Asa'ib Ahl Al-Haq in Iraq.

The Treasury Department said that "all property and interests in property of the designated or blocked persons described above that are in the United States or in the possession or control of US persons are blocked and must be reported to OFAC." 

"In addition, any entities that are owned, directly or indirectly, individually or in the aggregate, 50% or more by one or more blocked persons are also blocked," it added.

Hamas says top official's son dies of wounds sustained in Israeli airstrike

The Hamas group that rules the Gaza Strip and a hospital in the region both report that the son of a senior Hamas official has died of wound sustained in an Israeli drone strike. 

This follows reports of a drone strike on the Jabalia refugee camp on Wednesday. The hospital said two members of the Palestinian militant group, classified as a terrorist organization by the US, EU and others, were killed. An Israeli army spokesman said that the reports were being investigated. 

Azzam Khalil al-Hayya is a son of Khalil al-Hayya, a senior Hamas official and a key negotiator in the talks on a lasting peace settlement for the Gaza Strip. 

A Hamas spokesman told both the AFP and dpa agencies that Azzam was the fourth of al-Hayya's sons to be killed — with another killed last year during an Israeli strike on Qatar that targeted his father. Two more were reportedly killed in 2014 and 2008.

Skip next section Netanyahu says 'no terrorist immune' after Hezbollah commander killed in Beirut
05/07/2026May 7, 2026

Netanyahu says 'no terrorist immune' after Hezbollah commander killed in Beirut

The Israeli army said Thursday that a strike on Beirut's southern suburbs killed the commander of Hezbollah's "Radwan Force" unit a day earlier. It was the first Israeli strike on the Lebanese capital since a ceasefire was agreed last month.

There was no immediate confirmation from the Iran-backed Hezbollah. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defense Minister Israel Katz announced the attack in a joint statement on Wednesday.

On Thursday, Netanyahu said that the commander in question had been directing attacks against northern Israel.

"He believed he could continue directing attacks against our forces and our communities from his hidden terrorist headquarters in Beirut," Netanyahu said, adding that "no terrorist has immunity."

After Israel and the US attacked Iran, Hezbollah began launching attacks on northern Israel. The Israeli response has been to strike Hezbollah targets in Beirut and southern Lebanon, and deploy troops in what is calls a "security buffer zone" in a chunk of Lebanese territory south of the Litani River.

More than 2,700 people in Lebanon have been killed since hostilities resumed on March 2, according to the Lebanese Health Ministry. The Israeli military says Hezbollah has fired hundreds of rockets and drones at Israel during that time. Israel has said 17 soldiers have been killed in southern Lebanon since March 2, along with two civilians in northern Israel.

Iran's president says he met with supreme leader Khamenei

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said he met with supreme leader Mojtaba Khamenei, state media reported on Thursday. 

Khamenei has not been seen in public since the start of the Iran war, and is said to have been severely wounded in strikes that killed his father and predecessor, Ali Khamenei. Statements attributed to the supreme leader have been issued indirectly though state media. 

"What struck me most during this meeting was the vision and the humble and sincere approach of the supreme leader of the Islamic revolution," Pezeshkian said, without sharing further details of what was discussed, or when the meeting took place.

Skip next section Shell reports high first-quarter profits
05/07/2026May 7, 2026

Shell reports high first-quarter profits

Energy giant Shell on Thursday beat analyst expectations and reported profits of $6.92 billion (€5.87) for the first three months of 2026, which is nearly 25% higher than last year, and comes as the war in Iran has sent energy prices soaring.

In its earnings statement Shell said the strong results were "enabled by our relentless focus on operational performance in a quarter marked by unprecedented disruption in global energy markets."

French oil and gas giant TotalEnergies also recently reported a 51% jump in earnings during the January-March period.

The increase in oil company profits amid the soaring energy costs for consumers has been met with criticism.

"Fossil fuel giants are pocketing monstrous profits while drivers are being squeezed at the petrol pump and households are set to pay higher energy bills," said Danny Gross, climate campaigner at Friends of the Earth environmental organization.

The dramatic rise in energy prices has led to calls for the European Unionto introduce a so-called windfall tax on oil and gas companies, to use some of their profits to help governments fund relief schemes.

Iran denies attacking South Korean ship

Dmytro Hubenko Editor

Iran's embassy in Seoul on Thursday said it "firmly rejects and categorically denies" attacking a South Korean cargo ship in the Strait of Hormuz earlier this week.

The Panama-flagged HMM Namu was damaged by an explosion and fire on Monday while transiting the strategic waterway with 24 crew members on board.

US President Donald Trump said later that Iran had "taken some shots" at the vessel. He also urged South Korea to join now-paused US-led efforts to restore shipping through the strait.

Iran port organization offers services to stranded ships

Iran's Port and Maritime Organization has offered to provide general maritime services to commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's official Islamic Republic News Agency reported on Thursday morning.

The organization said it stood ready to provide ships with provisions, fuel, medical supplies and authorized items needed for repairs.

The message is set to be broadcast thrice daily for the next three days to ships in the region via UHF radio.

The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed. On Tuesday, the UAE accused Iran of firing at an oil tanker attempting to pass through the strait while on Wednesday, the US military fired at an Iranian oil tanker headed in the other direction.

Skip next section US State Department issues warning over Polymarket
05/07/2026May 7, 2026

US State Department issues warning over Polymarket

The US State Department has warned its staff not to use confidential information to place bets on sites like Polymarket and Kalshi, The Wall Street Journal reported.

"Recent press reports have raised concerns about U.S. government officials using nonpublic government information to place wagers on online prediction markets, such as Kalshi or Polymarket," the State Department memorandum reads.

"All employees are reminded that the misuse of nonpublic information by Department employees for financial benefit is a very serious offense and will not be tolerated."

Hundreds of millions of dollars has poured into bets about the US-Iran negotiations as well as other wagers regarding the war.

Several accounts won large sums after placing well-timed bets on US President Donald Trump's announcement of a ceasefire in April, prompting calls for closer scrutiny of such platforms.

Last month, a US soldier was charged with using classified information to place bets tied to the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro, winning $400,000 (€342,480) in the process.

Trump says Iran deal 'very possible'

US President Donald Trump has expressed optimism over the latest round of peace negotiations with Iran.

"We've had very good talks over the last 24 hours, and it's very possible that we'll make a deal," he told reporters in the Oval Office late on Wednesday.
