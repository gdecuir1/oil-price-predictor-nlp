Energy crisis persists as OPEC+ raises oil quotas for third time

OPEC+ lifts output targets again, but Iran conflict and Hormuz disruptions keep supply tight.

JAKARTA — The OPEC+ alliance has raised oil production quotas for a third consecutive time since the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, although the impact on global supply is seen as limited.

As reported by Reuters, during a virtual meeting on Sunday (3/5), seven core OPEC+ members agreed to increase output targets by 188,000 barrels per day for June 2026.

The move extends a monthly upward trend, though it does not necessarily translate into additional physical supply in the market.

The decision is viewed as a signal of OPEC+ readiness to boost supply if the conflict eases, while also demonstrating policy consistency following the exit of the United Arab Emirates from the alliance.

“OPEC+ is sending a dual message to the market: continuity despite the UAE’s exit, and control despite limited physical impact,” said Jorge Leon, an analyst at Rystad Energy and former OPEC official.

“Although production is rising on paper, the real impact on physical supply remains very limited given constraints in the Strait of Hormuz. This is not just about adding barrels, but signalling that OPEC+ remains in control,” he added.

Higher quotas are also reflected in Saudi Arabia, the group’s largest producer, with a production target of 10.291 million barrels per day for June — well above its previous output levels.

However, the Iran conflict, which has led to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz since 28 February, continues to constrain oil exports from Gulf producers, including Saudi Arabia, Iraq and Kuwait.

This has limited the ability of increased quotas to reach the market effectively.

Even if shipping routes reopen, industry players expect global oil distribution to take weeks to months to normalise.

Supply disruptions have pushed oil prices to four-year highs, surpassing USD 125 per barrel, raising concerns over potential jet fuel shortages and rising global inflation.

Recent data also showed a sharp decline in OPEC+ output in March, with the largest drops recorded in Iraq and Saudi Arabia due to export constraints.

OPEC+ is scheduled to meet again on 7 June to review its production policy. (DH/KR/ZH)
