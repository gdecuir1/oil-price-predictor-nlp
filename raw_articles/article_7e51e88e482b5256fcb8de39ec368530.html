Gas Prices Surge Over 30 Cents as Iran War Continues and Strait of Hormuz Remains Closed

By News Desk
Last updated: May 4, 2026
4 Min Read
Share

Gas prices across the United States have surged more than 30 cents per gallon over the past week, driven by ongoing tensions and disruptions caused by the conflict in Iran, which has led to the closure of the crucial Strait of Hormuz. As of Sunday, the national average price for regular gasoline stands at approximately $4.446 per gallon, a noticeable jump from $4.099 a week prior. This increase continues a trend seen since late February, just before the outbreak of hostilities when average prices were around $2.98. Comparatively, a year ago, drivers were paying an average of $3.171 per gallon.

According to the American Automobile Association (AAA), these current prices are the highest they’ve been since late July 2022. In light of the situation, former President Trump has made statements suggesting that gas prices will dramatically decrease once the conflict in Iran concludes. However, experts express uncertainty regarding both the timing of a resolution and the potential for prices to drop significantly, even after the Strait of Hormuz reopens.

Kevin Book, co-founder of ClearView Energy Partners, emphasized that the closure of the Strait—an essential passage for oil and natural gas shipping—will likely keep prices elevated. He noted that ongoing low inventories and extraction difficulties could lead to further increases until demand decreases. According to Book, the U.S. may face weeks or months of rising prices until stability is achieved in the market, as it could take considerable time to restore shipping routes, repair affected infrastructure, and replenish supplies.

He also warned that any future declines in gas prices might not signify a recovery. Instead, they might correlate with a recession that would decrease demand, effectively destabilizing the market. In response to escalating prices linked to the conflict, the U.S. Department of Energy has released 17.5 million barrels of crude oil from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve between March 20 and April 24, attempting to mitigate some of the financial pressure on consumers.

Additionally, seven member countries of the OPEC+ group announced on Sunday a commitment to increase oil production by 188,000 barrels per day starting in June, aiming to foster market stability amidst the volatility.

Further complicating the financial landscape, a weakening U.S. dollar presents challenges for American consumers. Analysis from Morgan Stanley indicates the dollar has depreciated approximately 10% from early January to late April, marking the steepest losses in the first half of a year since 1973. A weaker dollar has the dual effect of heightening the cost of foreign travel and raising prices for imported goods, although it may provide some advantages for American exporters in international markets.

With these economic factors at play, many Americans are feeling the direct impact at the gas pump, leading to heightened concerns about overall affordability and financial stability in the weeks ahead.
