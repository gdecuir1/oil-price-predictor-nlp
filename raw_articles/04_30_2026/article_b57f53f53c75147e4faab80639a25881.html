Kazakhstan sticking with OPEC

Did Kremlin have hand in decision not to withdraw?

Apr 30, 2026
(Photo: gov.kz)

Defying some analysts’ expectations, Kazakhstan has opted not to follow the United Arab Emirates in abandoning OPEC’s oil production quotas.

The Kazakh Energy Ministry announced April 29 that “the issue of changing the format of the country’s participation in the alliance is not on the agenda.” Kazakhstan cooperates with OPEC under the OPEC+ platform, which coordinates production between full OPEC members and other oil-producing states with the aim of optimizing energy supplies and prices.

The UAE announced its withdrawal from OPEC as of May 1, a move that raises questions about the cartel’s future ability to influence global energy prices.

Some industry experts in Kazakhstan had called on Astana to follow the UAE’s example, due mainly to the fact that the country has routinely exceeded production quotas in recent years.

“We are not a member of OPEC, and there is no responsibility (penalties) for excess oil production ... It would be better for Kazakhstan to withdraw from OPEC+,” industry analyst Abzal Narymbetov wrote on his telegram channel.

Kazakhstan is currently subject to an OPEC+ framework created last year to compensate for overproduction. So far, however, the country has struggled to meet its compensation requirements, the Interfax news agency has reported. Kazakh officials say western energy giants, including Chevron, complicate efforts to adhere to OPEC+ limits because the companies have a contractually controlling interest in production at major Kazakh oil fields and prioritize profits over production quotas. 

The Kazakh state budget is also heavily reliant on the profits generated by energy exports. The vast majority of Kazakh oil is exported via Russia along the Caspian Pipeline Consortium route. Given Kazakhstan’s dependence on Russia for exports, the Kremlin’s wishes may have factored into Astana’s decision to stick with OPEC+.

Russia has firmly backed OPEC following the UAE’s withdraw. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told journalists in Moscow on April 29 that “this format helps to substantially, let’s say, minimize fluctuations in energy markets.” Russia, which is reaping desperately needed windfall profits from energy exports due to the continuing turmoil in the Persian Gulf, is eager to avoid an oil export price war, which is a potential outcome of the UAE’s withdrawal from OPEC.
