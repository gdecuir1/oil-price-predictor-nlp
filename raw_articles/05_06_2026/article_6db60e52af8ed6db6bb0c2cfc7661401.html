Is It Too Late To Consider Scorpio Tankers (STNG) After A 112% One-Year Rally?

May 07, 2026
Simply Wall St
Reviewed by Bailey Pemberton
  • Investors may be wondering if Scorpio Tankers at around US$82.56 is still offering value after a strong run, or if most of the opportunity is already priced in.
  • The stock has returned 1.9% over the last week, 7.9% over the last 30 days, 66.2% year to date and 112.4% over the past year. This naturally raises questions about how much risk and reward is now baked into the price.
  • Recent coverage has focused on Scorpio Tankers within the broader oil and gas shipping space, including commentary on product tanker supply and demand, charter rate trends and how these factors can influence investor sentiment toward the stock. This context helps explain why some investors are reassessing whether the current share price fairly reflects those industry conditions.
  • On Simply Wall St's valuation checks, Scorpio Tankers has a score of 5/6. The rest of this article will walk through the main valuation approaches behind that score, and then finish with a way to tie them together into a clearer view of what the stock might be worth.

Approach 1: Scorpio Tankers Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Analysis

A Discounted Cash Flow, or DCF, model estimates what a stock could be worth by projecting the cash the company may generate in the future and then discounting those cash flows back to today.

For Scorpio Tankers, the model used is a 2 Stage Free Cash Flow to Equity approach, based on recent free cash flow of about $539.3 million. Analysts provide specific forecasts for several years and, after that, Simply Wall St extends the projections using its own assumptions to build a ten year path for free cash flow. Within that path, projected free cash flow for 2028 is $432 million, with further estimates running through to 2035.

Bringing all those projected cash flows back to today produces an estimated intrinsic value of about $112.73 per share. Compared with the recent share price of around $82.56, the DCF output suggests the stock is trading at roughly a 26.8% discount. On this model alone, this indicates that Scorpio Tankers may be undervalued.

Result: UNDERVALUED

Approach 2: Scorpio Tankers Price vs Earnings

For profitable companies, the P/E ratio is a useful yardstick because it links what you pay directly to the earnings the business is currently generating. Higher growth expectations and lower perceived risk typically justify a higher P/E, while slower growth or higher risk usually point to a lower, more cautious multiple.

Scorpio Tankers currently trades on a P/E of about 7.5x. That sits below the Oil and Gas industry average of roughly 13.9x and also below the peer group average of about 16.3x, which may catch your eye if you are comparing stocks within the sector.

Simply Wall St’s Fair Ratio for Scorpio Tankers is 11.0x. This is a proprietary estimate of what a reasonable P/E might be for the company, after factoring in elements such as earnings growth, profit margins, industry, market cap and company specific risks. Because it adjusts for these characteristics, the Fair Ratio can give a more tailored reference point than a simple comparison with broad industry or peer averages.

Set against the current P/E of 7.5x, the Fair Ratio of 11.0x suggests Scorpio Tankers trades below that tailored benchmark.

Result: UNDERVALUED
