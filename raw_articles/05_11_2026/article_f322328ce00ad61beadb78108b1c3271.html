Chinese Independent Refiners Slash Fuel Output Amid Surge in Crude Costs

Caleb Nathaniel
Published May 12, 2026 - 12:49
Last Update May 12, 2026 - 12:49

Independent refiners in China's Shandong province are reducing fuel production as plummeting margins and high crude costs driven by the Iran war strain operations. These processors, as reported by Businesstimes, are currently navigating a combination of weak domestic demand and a surplus of refined products.

Operational rates at these facilities have dropped to approximately 50 percent, down from 55 percent in April. This decline persists despite instructions from Beijing for small refiners, often called teapots, to maintain output levels to ensure domestic supply stability during global disruptions.

Financial pressures have intensified, with estimates suggesting losses between 500 yuan and 600 yuan for every tonne of crude processed during the final week of April. Data from SCI further indicates that April losses averaged 649 yuan per tonne, a sharp reversal from the 269 yuan profit recorded a year earlier.

The current situation has forced several smaller plants to halt operations entirely. According to an anonymous source familiar with the matter, the financial strain has become unsustainable for many operators.

"Without cutting output, the losses are unbearable," said a third source.

This individual noted that several refineries have already scaled back their run rates by five to 10 percentage points compared to April levels. The surge in costs is largely attributed to the disappearance of cheap crude stockpiles and the rising premiums on Russian and Iranian barrels following the closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

Government oversight remains a significant factor as the National Development and Reform Commission previously warned that failing to maintain production could result in reduced import quotas. On May 9, several refiners formally requested permission from the Shandong provincial government to lower processing rates or suspend specific units.

The regional government has not yet approved these requests. It remains uncertain if the independent refiners will continue their current production cuts if official authorization is withheld by Beijing authorities.
