Market performance in the shipping sector has seen a significant boost following disruptions in the Middle East, according to reports from Detik Finance. These events have effectively doubled voyage lengths as Atlantic Basin flows move to replace lost regional volumes.

Management identifies a strong run-up in the Atlantic market as a primary driver for accelerating momentum. This trend has significantly drained vessel supply in the East, resulting in a pronounced global supply-demand imbalance.

The company is currently implementing a through-the-cycle value creation strategy. This approach focuses on balancing fleet expansion with higher capital returns now that major capital expenditure requirements for 2025 are finalized.

Future Market Outlook and Fleet Expansion

Management anticipates sustained strength in the market due to urgent post-conflict inventory restocking. Long-term structural shifts are also expected as refining capacity continues to migrate toward Eastern regions.

A newbuilding program featuring two Handysize tankers is underway with deliveries set for late 2028. This initiative addresses an aging global fleet, where 50 percent of MR vessels are projected to exceed 20 years of age within the next five years.

The company maintains a high sensitivity to earnings growth. Management noted that every $10,000 per day increase in Time Charter Equivalent (TCE) rates contributes approximately $2 per share to annual earnings.

Capital Allocation and Risk Management

Following a period of intense reinvestment, the company has doubled its quarterly dividend payout ratio to two-thirds of adjusted earnings. This shift reflects a new priority toward enhancing shareholder returns.

Strategic risks remain a focal point, particularly the potential closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Such a disruption currently threatens 15 percent of global oil product flows and 30 percent of crude oil flows.

Vessel acquisitions made in the previous year have already shown strong appreciation. The company reported that these assets have increased in value by approximately 30 percent to 35 percent on a like-for-like basis.
