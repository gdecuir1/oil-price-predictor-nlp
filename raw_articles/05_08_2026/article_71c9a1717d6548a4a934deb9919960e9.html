Libya's Zawiya refinery shut down after clashes: two dead, oil port evacuated – video

With a refining capacity of around 120 thousand barrels per day, the plant is one of the main fuel production centres in Tripolitania.

Tripoli
,
8 May 2026

    A man and a woman were killed in armed clashes that have been ongoing since early this morning near the Zawiya refinery in western Libya. The clashes occurred between a group led by Mohammed Bahroun, known as "Al Far" (the Rat), and other armed factions. The Libyan news portal "Fawasel" reported this. The clashes began in the early hours of today following the announcement of a large-scale security operation launched by the Zawiya Security Directorate, along with the "joint security room," against armed groups, traffickers, and wanted individuals accused of crimes ranging from migrant smuggling and drug trafficking to fuel and weapons smuggling. Local authorities described it as a targeted campaign targeting "criminal gangs" and "outlaws" active in the city.

    Subsequently, the Azzawiya Oil Refining Company, a subsidiary of the National Oil Corporation (NOC), announced that the Zawiya refinery had completely suspended operations and ordered the evacuation of the oil port due to the clashes. In a statement, the NOC subsidiary explained that "several heavy artillery shells" had hit "various areas within the company, reaching the operational zones." According to the company, the decision to "completely shut down the refinery" and "clear the port of tankers" was made "to preserve the safety of workers, facilities, and the surrounding environment," while armed clashes continue near the oil complex. The company also announced the activation of an "emergency committee" to monitor the situation of employees still present within the facilities and ensure "their safety and essential needs."

    In the statement, the company expressed "deep concern" about the deteriorating security situation and urgently appealed to "all parties to the conflict" to declare an immediate ceasefire and keep the oil installations out of the hostilities. "Any threat to the security of these vital facilities represents a threat to the resources of the entire Libyan people," the statement read.

    The Zawiya refinery is one of western Libya's most strategic energy assets. With a refining capacity of approximately 120 barrels per day, the plant is one of Tripolitania's main fuel production centers and is connected to the main fields in Fezzan, including Sharara and the El Feel ("Elephant") area, jointly operated by NOC and Eni. The city of Zawiya, approximately 45 kilometers west of Tripoli, has for years been one of the main hubs of western Libya's illegal economies, driven by fuel smuggling, migrant trafficking, and the presence of rival armed groups.
