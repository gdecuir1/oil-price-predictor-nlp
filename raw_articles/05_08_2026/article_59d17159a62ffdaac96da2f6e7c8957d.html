Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V.: Iraqi Kurdistan Sarqala field under Gazprom management

Constantin Macri 08/05/2026 | 19:04
Twitter Facebook Linkedin

The Sarqala oil deposit inside Iraqi Kurdistan’s Chamchamal District serves as the sole producing asset for Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V., an upstream arm domiciled in the Netherlands belonging to the Russian Gazprom Group. Through a production-sharing structure, the firm manages all field operations, having assumed a complete operational control in late February 2016.

 

A restructuring completed in February 2025 placed Gazprom Neft Middle East under the indirect ownership of PJSC Gazprom, held through a series of intermediate holding entities. Prior to that, the company formed part of the Gazprom Neft group.

The organisation is also registered under the trade name Garmian Petroleum in the Dutch commercial register.

Field facilities and subsurface setting

Sarqala is located roughly 100 kilometres southeast of Kirkuk, within the Chamchamal District of Sulaymaniyah Governorate, inside the Zagros fold belt.

From a geological standpoint, the reservoir lies within the intensely deformed Zagros fold-and-thrust belt, an area characterised by extensive faulting and compartmentalised traps. Production is drawn from the Jeribe carbonate formation. The crude is light, close to 40o API, and sweet, containing only trace amounts of sulphur.

Field output is currently delivered from four producing wells. The associated surface facilities, operated by Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V., include a processing plant with a nameplate capacity of 35,000 barrels of crude oil and 70 million cubic feet of natural gas per day. Since 2022, the separated gas has been routed to a nearby power facility operated by Aggreko company, supplying electricity to surrounding communities.

This configuration was reached through execution of a phased development programme. Two wells were put into operation during the early stage. Output increased further following the commissioning of the third well, Sarqala-3, which entered service in 2019. The well was drilled to a depth of 3,921 metres and had an estimated production ceiling of 12,000 barrels per day. Its start-up lifted total field production by approximately 25 percent, bringing output to around 35,000 barrels per day.

The drilling campaign was carried out under technically challenging conditions. Formation pressure was abnormally high and downhole temperatures were extreme. Addressing these constraints required a set of specialised engineering solutions, including the use of large-diameter casing strings with high-strength premium connections, high-density drilling fluids to maintain wellbore stability and ensure hole cleaning, and cement systems incorporating mineral and iron-oxide additives.

The increase in production was accompanied by a corresponding expansion of surface infrastructure. Additional separation trains for both high- and low-pressure streams were installed, two storage tanks with a capacity of 17,000 barrels each were constructed, and the truck loading systems were upgraded.

Field works’ execution was carried out by an international team with workers from approximately 20 countries. As development progressed, the operator also engaged external contractors. In October 2017, a three-year master services agreement was signed with Petrofac company following a competitive tender, covering engineering support, maintenance, and operational services on a call-off basis. In parallel, DQE International (a drilling contractor) was selected through a separate tender to perform drilling operations on the Garmian and Shakal blocks.

 

Corporate history and ownership

Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. was established in 2012 to pursue upstream opportunities in the self‐governing Kurdish territory in the northern parts of Iraq.

In July of that year, a Production Sharing Contract (PSC) was finalised among the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG), Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V., and WesternZagros limited (WZ), a Cyprus‐based company.

The PSC expires in 2033, with a possible five‐year extension, and grants Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. an interest that continues to be its core asset.

Initially, WZ acted as the operator of the Garmian block, a role it retained until the end of February of 2016. In that year, Gazprom Neft Middle East took over operatorship from WZ and assumed command of well engineering, reservoir supervision, and facility improvements. From the end of February of 2016 onwards, Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. has continued as the sole operator.

While operational strength was being built, the ownership framework was evolving. On 24 February 2025, a Russian intermediary entity, then called GPN‑Middle East Projects LLC, was placed under Gazprom Middle East LLC and was later renamed to West Asia LLC. Public filings in Russia confirm that Gazprom Middle East LLC is entirely owned by PJSC Gazprom through Gazprom Assets Management LLC and Gazprom Capital LLC. Gazprom Middle East LLC, in turn, controls West Asia LLC, the immediate owner of Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V.

This restructuring removed PJSC Gazprom Neft from the ownership chain of Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. and placed the company under the indirect control of the Gazprom parent. As a result, Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. now forms part of Gazprom’s international upstream portfolio as an indirectly held asset.

 

Participating stakes, spending split, and output allocation

Under the Garmian PSC, Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. and WZ each possess a 40 percent participating share, with the remaining 20 percent carried by the KRG. Both corporate partners divide all capital and operational spending equally, meaning Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. finances half of the total project costs.

The PSC entitles Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. to receive its share of crude oil in kind and market it independently. Over the project’s entire span, however, the actual sales mechanisms have been shaped by shifting regional export practices, as the following section describes.

 

How crude sales and offtake developed

For most of the field’s operational history, crude oil was sold under Lifting Agreements with the KRG’s Ministry of Natural Resources. Prices were tied to Brent benchmark.

Export operations were abruptly halted in March 2023, when the Iraqi-Turkish cross‐border pipeline to the Turkish Mediterranean coast was shut down. In the following weeks, Gazprom Neft Middle East worked with the KRG authorities to secure an alternative channel: rather than exporting, the firm began supplying its crude to a local entity, Taurus Arm, which processed the oil inside Iraq. This tactical shift allowed Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. to keep generating cash even though international shipments had come to a halt.

The Baghdad government subsequently transformed the fiscal environment. On 2 February 2025, the Iraqi parliament amended the 2023‐2025 federal budget, instituting a temporary flat compensation rate of $16 for every barrel placed into the Kurdish pipeline network. Once the pipeline restarted, under this system the federal authorities transferred the corresponding sums to Erbil, and the KRG allocated them among the international operators, including Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V..

The mechanism is transitional and will be replaced upon completion of an independent field‐level audit of production and transport costs. Wood Mackenzie, the consultancy agency jointly appointed by the Iraqi and Kurdish authorities, is performing the review, and its findings will directly impact future earnings of Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V.

Before the pipeline restarted, crude kept flowing to Taurus Arm under the local sales scheme. In September 2025, following quadrilateral negotiations between Erbil, Baghdad, Turkish officials, and the international producers, exports resumed. From that point, the operator once again received the $16‑per‑barrel interim rate while the audit advances. Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. continues to operate under this temporary pricing framework.

 

Social and communities-related activities in the Garmian region

Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. has made significant contributions to various social initiatives in the regions where it operates. The principal areas of its involvement are listed below.

  • A scholarship fund allowed 19 students of Kurdish origin to enrol in petroleum-related programmes at two prestigious Russian educational institutions: Saint Petersburg Mining University and Gubkin Russian State University of Oil and Gas in Moscow. Twelve of those graduates later joined the workforce of Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. In September 2025, the enterprise distributed schoolbags and stationery to 130 pupils in the villages of Hasira and Mil Qasim, an initiative organised by Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. Environmental classes were delivered in April 2025, and a practical workshop on household electrical safety was held in January 2026.
  • In Nassala, Hasira, and Mil Qasim villages, three brand-new football pitches were constructed, providing local communities with the infrastructure for the beautiful game. The region’s football clubs were outfitted with comprehensive sets of playing equipment, while additional sports amenities were developed, including facilities for boxing and tennis, complemented by the creation of two vibrant playgrounds.
  • Two fully fitted ambulances, together with dental and ultrasound apparatus and protective gear, were donated to clinics in the towns of Kalar and Kifri. Mobile health services operated in hamlets adjacent to the firm’s installations throughout 2020‐2021.
  • Two schoolhouses in Hasira and Mil Qasim were completed in 2023 under the oversight of Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. These community‐directed investments have persisted through the fluctuations in the Kurdish oil sector and were unaffected by the 2025 change in corporate control.

 

Frequently asked questions

1) What asset does Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. operate and where is it located?

The company operates the Sarqala field in the Garmian block of Iraqi Kurdistan, located in the Chamchamal District of Sulaymaniyah Governorate, approx. 100 km southeast of Kirkuk.

2) What is the current ownership structure of Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V.?

The company is indirectly owned by PJSC Gazprom through a chain of Russian holding companies, following its removal from the Gazprom Neft group in 2025.

3) Does PJSC Gazprom Neft own Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V.?

No. As of 2026, Gazprom Neft neither owns nor controls Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V.

4) What agreement governs operations at the Sarqala field?

Operations are governed by a Production Sharing Contract (PSC) signed by Gazprom Neft Middle East B.V. in 2012 with the Kurdistan Regional Government and WesternZagros limited. The agreement runs until 2033, with a possible extension.

5) How has production developed at the Sarqala field?

Production increased in stages, as additional wells became operational, including Sarqala-3 in 2019, reaching approx. 35,000 barrels of production per day.

6) What are the characteristics of the crude oil produced at Sarqala?

The crude is light, with an API gravity of around 400, and has low sulphur content. Production comes from the Jeribe carbonate formation.

7) How is oil from the Sarqala field marketed?

Crude has mainly been marketed through arrangements with the Kurdistan Regional Government, with pricing linked to Brent. After the 2023 pipeline shutdown, part of the output was redirected to domestic processing before exports resumed. As of 2026, the crude produced from the Sarqala field is being delivered to export through the Iraqi-Turkish pipeline, to the Turkish Mediterranean port of Ceyhan.
