Iran: Houthis Will Close the Bab el-Mandeb Strait

30.04.2026 21:20

A worrying statement came from Iran, which continues to keep the Strait of Hormuz, of great importance for global oil trade, closed. The statement said, 'The Houthis in Yemen will close the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.'

Iranian officials continue to keep the Strait of Hormuz, the heart of global oil trade, closed, while signaling a new move through their Houthi allies. The announcement stated that the Houthis in Yemen would also close the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the gateway to the Red Sea, to traffic.

CONCERN GROWS IN MARKETS

This situation has created the risk of both of the most critical points in global energy supply being simultaneously taken offline. The possibility of strategic waterways being closed has caused great concern in world markets, with experts warning of sharp increases in oil prices.

This blockage in both the Persian Gulf and the Red Sea route could bring not only energy transport but also general trade traffic between Asia and Europe to a standstill. The global economy may face one of the biggest logistics crises in its history.

"THEY ARE CONDUCTING EXERCISES IN BAB EL-MANDEB"

Meanwhile, Iranian parliament member and member of the Iranian Parliament's National Security Commission, Ali Hazrian, stated that the Yemeni Armed Forces have recently been conducting exercises to block the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and preparations for its closure are complete.

"THERE ARE TWO OPTIONS FOR SHIPS"

Commenting on international maritime traffic, Hezriyan said there are two options for non-hostile ships: "Ships will either bypass the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, incurring an additional cost of approximately $30 million, or pay $5 million to Yemeni forces to obtain a safe passage permit."

Hezriyan emphasized that this step has come up with the aim of managing transit costs in the region and ensuring strategic dominance, noting that it also carries a message of control over energy and trade routes in the area.

THE IMPORTANCE OF THE BAB EL-MANDEB STRAIT

The Bab el-Mandeb Strait, connecting the Red Sea with the Gulf of Aden (and thus the Indian Ocean), is one of the world's most critical "choke points" for global trade and energy supply security. Serving as the main gateway for maritime traffic between Europe and Asia via the Suez Canal, this strait hosts thousands of oil tankers and container ships each year, forming the transit route for approximately 10% of global crude oil trade. Geopolitically located between the Horn of Africa and the Arabian Peninsula, this strategic passage has the potential to cause major disruptions in global supply chains and sharp fluctuations in energy prices in the event of any blockage or security crisis; therefore, the region remains the focus of military and diplomatic interest from major powers.
