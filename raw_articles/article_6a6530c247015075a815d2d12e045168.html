Guyana, going from frontier to global oil force

  • May 6, 2026
  • Chevron, CNOOC, ExxonMobil, global oil, Guyana, Stabroek Block

(By Oil & Gas 360) – A decade ago, Guyana wasn’t part of the global oil conversation. Today, it’s one of the most important new sources of supply in the world.

Few countries have moved from discovery to production as quickly or as successfully. What started as a high-risk exploration play has become a case study in how modern oil development can work when geology, capital, and execution align.

The turning point came in 2015 with the first major discovery in the Stabroek Block. What followed was not just a series of successful wells, but a scale of resource confirmation that fundamentally changed expectations. Billions of barrels were identified across multiple fields, creating one of the largest offshore discoveries in recent history.

What sets Guyana apart is not just the size of the resource, but how quickly it has been developed.

Production moved from zero to more than 600,000 barrels per day in just a few years, with a clear trajectory toward more than 1 million barrels per day later this decade.

Execution has been the differentiator.

At the center of the story is an operator group led by ExxonMobil, alongside Hess Corporation (now being acquired by Chevron) and CNOOC. Together, they control the prolific Stabroek Block, with Exxon as operator holding about 45%, Hess/Chevron 30%, and CNOOC 25%.

This consortium has set the pace, fast-tracking projects, controlling costs, and scaling infrastructure alongside production. Floating production, storage, and offloading vessels (FPSOs) have been deployed in phases, allowing output to grow in a structured and repeatable way.

Beyond the core consortium, other majors are positioning around the basin. TotalEnergies has exposure in nearby acreage, and additional independents and smaller players are exploring adjacent blocks, reinforcing Guyana’s status as a broader basin play rather than a single-asset story.

Capital followed certainty.

Guyana has attracted tens of billions of dollars in investment, with more than $50 billion already committed to multiple offshore developments. The key difference from most frontier regions is that the pathway from discovery to cash flow has already been proven.

But rapid growth brings its own challenges. The country is navigating how to manage a sudden influx of oil revenue while building institutions, infrastructure, and regulatory capacity.

Questions around fiscal terms, local content, and long-term economic diversification are becoming more central as production expands.

At the same time, geopolitical attention is increasing.

Guyana’s rise has shifted regional dynamics, particularly given its proximity to Venezuela and the long-standing territorial dispute over the Essequibo region. While development has continued uninterrupted, the situation adds a layer of complexity to an otherwise stable investment environment.

Despite that, Guyana remains one of the clearest growth stories in global energy.

It offers large-scale resources, competitive development costs, and a defined production trajectory, all backed by some of the world’s largest and most capable operators.

In a market where new supply is increasingly difficult to bring online, that combination stands out.

Guyana is no longer a frontier bet. It is a producing basin with momentum, visibility, and scale.

And increasingly, it is a proving ground not just for resources, but for how global capital, oil majors, and governments come together to build the next generation of supply.
