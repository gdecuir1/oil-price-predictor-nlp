Saudi Economic Growth Slows as Iran War Hits Oil Exports

Saudi Arabia’s quarterly economic growth rate slowed to its lowest since mid-2024, as the kingdom grapples with the Iran war’s effects on its crucial oil industry.

Article content

The figures give some of the first detailed signs of the economic impact on Saudi Arabia of the US-Israeli war on Iran that began Feb. 28. The Islamic Republic retaliated with strikes on US allies across the Gulf, including targeting energy facilities in Saudi Arabia, disrupting production and exports.

Article content

The conflict effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint through which a fifth of the world’s oil typically transits. Saudi Arabia has managed to offset some of the losses by redirecting flows via a pipeline to the Red Sea port of Yanbu. That has it allowed it to partially benefit from the spike in oil prices, which reached a war-time high of $126 a barrel on Thursday.

Article content

“The playbook that the Saudi authorities deployed at the beginning of the crisis allowed them to be more resilient,” Jihad Azour, the International Monetary Fund’s Middle East and Central Asia director, said in an interview with Bloomberg TV this month. 

Article content
Article content

The IMF expects Saudi Arabia’s economy to grow 3.1% this year — a downward revision of 0.9 percentage points from the lender’s October projection. The fund sees the kingdom as taking a smaller hit than some of its neighbors.

Article content

That resilience may face a new test with the United Arab Emirates’ exit from OPEC after six decades of membership. 

Article content

The departure of Saudi’s neighbor on May 1 is a significant blow to the Saudi-led group and its ability to manage oil prices by adjusting supply. The shock move is the culmination of years of tension with the kingdom over oil output policy and competition for regional political influence.
